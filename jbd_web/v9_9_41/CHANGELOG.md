# V9.9.41 — Measured support balance, damage feedback and staged vehicle fires

All five strikes and five reinforcement tiers are compared with actual game updates, projectile collisions, arrival time, aiming, magazines and reloads. A reproducible benchmark runs three seeds against a single stationary infantry target, a stationary unarmored trooptruck and a shared three-infantry-plus-truck cluster. The thirty-second window includes reinforcement entry and movement. Test targets have 100,000 HP and the test driver is protected against early removal. Targets do not move or return fire. These are potential damage comparisons rather than predictions of live kills. Reinforcements can continue fighting after the window. Full results and methodology are in balance.html, BALANCE.md and balance-results.json; support_balance.cjs regenerates the measurements and verifies the relative balance gates.

The strafing aircraft receives a 64-round budget, two visible wing-gun tracers per burst, correct muzzle-to-target aiming, a six-second pass and target rotation. Raw damage is 9 per infantry hit or 5.5 per vehicle hit. Bombs use two blasts at 78 infantry/105 vehicle damage each. Mortars use three rounds at 28/32; eight howitzer rounds use 100/150, with the opening shells concentrated around the selected point. Sixteen barrage rounds use 125/230 over a broader area. In the common cluster benchmark the MG run deals about 71% more damage than the cheaper bombing run, and regimental fire deals about 42% more than the howitzer battery. Precision and area damage retain different strengths.

The five-person MG team gains accuracy and modest projectile damage; the former large elite spike is reduced. Scout cars have stronger real projectiles, more effective accuracy, greater engagement reach and component effects against vehicles. They turn to another target after the driver is disabled. Their hull damage remains limited by the small-arms floor; explosives or fuel fire finish the vehicle. A tank uses actual cannon shells plus coaxial MG fire and can shoot during its approach. Allied vehicle firing cadence is independent of enemy difficulty, avoiding weaker allied support on Easy.

Starting MG magazine: 48 instead of 40. Cycle: 0.085 seconds instead of 0.10, approximately 706 rather than 600 rounds per minute. Manual tap/hold input, upgraded capacity and reload progression remain.

Vehicle damage gets two coalesced, colored, outlined labels lasting 2.4 seconds, with component progress, glass/radiator/tyre/fuel/body/frame descriptions and a brief colored hull outline. Mild burning hulls display a crew-rescue warning.

Fuel ignition from gunfire requires more penetrating fuel-component hits, at least six seconds of leak age, a sufficiently developed pool and further sparks with lower probability. Sustained hits can still ignite the fuel; time alone cannot skip the hit requirement. Fire chooses one of three random paths: about 20% rapid full explosion, 45% one small vehicle pop and burn-through, 35% two small vehicle pops and burn-through. Spilled fuel may produce additional delayed ignition puffs. Small pops use quieter recorded explosions and compact fireballs. They spread visible fuel pools that can ignite later. Staged fires expend their energy and finish as burnt-out hulls; they do not always add a final full-size blast. The direct full fuel explosion is the most destructive surrounding blast. Each destroyed vehicle awards one kill credit, and consumed wrecks cannot repeat the same explosion.

Healthy crew evacuate and flee. A small pop can leave one or two living casualties trapped at a safe rear access edge. Both enemy and allied medics can approach a lightly burning hull, release a casualty, physically drag them beyond the danger radius and only then provide normal treatment. Normal healing cannot treat a trapped passenger through the hull. Flames, severe wounds and death can interrupt rescue; a casualty dropped by a dead rescuer stays at their actual position. Medics remain vulnerable and cannot revive the dead.

Verification: syntax, complete battle regression and three-seed support balance gates pass. Added tests cover starting magazine/cadence, all fire modes, one-credit destruction, smaller staged collateral versus the full explosion, unlit spill growth and later ignition, stricter fuel aging/spark gates, actual enemy and allied crew extraction/dragging/treatment, healthy crew flight, component notices, trapped-treatment restrictions, failed-drag position and identical allied vehicle firing cadence across Easy/Random/Hard. Previous support UI, maps, vehicle roster, two-grenade finishing, penetration, scout persistence, manual controls, audio, gore and medic tests remain. No physical-device playtest was performed.
